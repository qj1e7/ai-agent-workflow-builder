      Rules slides 4 and 29

      • We may start early, but the project must not end before 18 Apr 2027.
      • After accepting the project, changing or withdrawing it costs grades.
      • When something is unclear, it is our job to ask the lecturers or the sponsor. The briefing came from Darryl Ng, darrylng@nus.edu.sg.

      What the project must show slides 3 and 4

      • Bring together what we studied, and use its tools, techniques and methods to architect and build a smart system that is secure and scalable.
      • Add value beyond the basics. The lecturers expect it in areas such as agentic AI, LLMs, machine learning, analytics, safety, real-time behaviour or fault tolerance.
      • Cover the areas of a few of our certificates.
      • Focus on the architecture and on building a significant slice of it. The architecture should allow for a few releases. The slice can target an MVP first.

            Evidence rules slides 28 and 29

            • Do not just claim an activity happened. Show evidence, briefly in the presentation and in more detail in the report, for one or more architecturally significant use cases.
            • Without evidence in the presentation and the report, the assessors cannot give marks for the activity.
            • Go beyond an architecture overview diagram. Show the functional elements, how they pack into subsystems, and how the subsystems deploy onto nodes.
            • Give a use case diagram as the overview of the functional requirements.
            • Give class and sequence diagrams, at class and object level, for the significant or complex use cases. Design means more than storage. Show how each use case is built with the frontend, backend and communication frameworks.
            • When we explain how we solved an architecture or design problem, show the problem and the solution in detail, with diagrams, not only in words.
            • Confidentiality is no reason to skip evidence. Use synthetic data for the presentation and the submission.
            • Be ready to show these highlights and the ones from the project guidelines the lecturers announced earlier.
            • Apply the techniques and knowledge from our MTech courses.

                    Reference

                    Proposal scope

                    What our approved proposal (v0.2) promised. The lecturers hold us to it. Tick an item when it is met. Where the plan changed course, the report should say what changed and why.

                    Each note says where an item stands today. In place means it exists in the repo. Changed means the plan moved away from the proposal. Gap means it is missing. Check means someone needs to confirm it.

                    Sponsor needs

                    What CODESparks needs from the product, and the safety rules that come from the sponsor and our October feasibility test. We never weaken a safety rule without an ADR.

                    How we work

                    Every week

                    • Log your hours per task, estimated and actual.
                    • Move your issues on the board. The burndown chart comes from it, and the guideline asks for daily updates.
                    • Save evidence as it happens. Pipeline runs, scan reports and load test results go in docs/evidence/.

                    Every sprint

                    • Sprints are two weeks long. The proposal promised twelve, four in Phase 1 (October and November) and eight in Phase 2 (December to March).
                    • Plan the sprint from the product backlog, hold a short daily stand-up, then a sprint review and a retro. Save the notes.

                    Every month

                    • Send the team lead your hours before the progress report.
                    • The report holds the sprint backlog, every member's hours since the last report, problems that need the lecturers' help, and next month's plan.

                    Always

                    • Work on a branch and open a PR to main. Never push to main.
                    • Never commit secrets, keys or tokens. .env files stay out of git.
                    • Build from the prototype. docs/design/prototypes/ is out of date.
                    • Use the names in GLOSSARY.md, never the old themed names.
                    • A big decision gets an ADR in docs/adr/ before its code lands.

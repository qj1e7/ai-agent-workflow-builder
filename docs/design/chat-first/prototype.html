Redrawn on 22 Nov 2026: Mission control, light (ADR 0052)

  • A dark rail holds Home, Workflows and the Watchlist, with Settings and the person at its foot. The top bar is gone. Home’s count is the Needs you count.
  • Home is the Planner’s box, then Needs you, then a board of workflow tiles. Each tile shows its state, its next run, and its last run as a line of steps.
  • After a question, Home becomes a chat with the box at the bottom and a slim list docked on the right: the Needs you count, then each workflow’s name with its state mark. “‹ Home” or Home in the rail goes back to the board, and the chat is kept. In a narrower window the board gives way to the pinned line. Something opened from a card still shows beside the chat, in the board’s place.
  • Workflows is filter chips, Needs you, then one table with each last run as steps. A workflow page draws its steps as a light diagram with today’s run on it.
  • A run’s page is a light stage: the agents’ faces on the steps, hand-offs as glowing dots, a pulsing amber gate with a timer, and a red stop with the AI troubleshooter’s card. It plays once on open, and Replay plays it again. With reduced motion, nothing moves.
  • Switch “New words” to Show to see every label or sentence that is new against the sketch of 13 Nov.

What’s new in version 3

  • Where you came from decides. From the rail, Workflows and the Watchlist open as full pages, and the chat shows only on Home. From a card in the chat, the first click opens beside it, and anything you open from there goes full page, with Back to Home. A flow shows once: while it is open beside the chat, the card doesn’t repeat it. Play “Browse”, then “Morning check”.
  • The side panel is a summary: what it is, its state, and the next step. More details opens the full page, which adds what helps you understand it, such as a run’s step-by-step log or a release’s notes. An approval always shows everything that will be written.
  • Every part of a request gets a tick or a cross, with a reason. Nothing is dropped, and a missing time is asked, never guessed.
  • “Make the React check run on Mondays only” changes the workflow you have. You see before and after, and can undo.
  • A run tells its story. Moving dashes flow into the step that is working, and turn slow and amber while the run waits for a yes. The line into a step that stopped turns red. Retry picks up at the step that stopped, and a slow step shows how long it has been working. The yes shows where the request went. The new ticket pops out at the end, and a finished run settles into one line. Play “Morning check”, then switch to Bob and approve.
  • The Planner’s test run flows the same way, block to block in n8n. Type “watch Kubernetes every Monday at 9am and create a Jira ticket”.
  • A stopped run answers three things: what happened, what was affected, and whether a retry is safe.
  • Bob sees what will be written and what wasn’t checked, can ask Alice a question, and can decline with a reason. A declined record never says “created”.
  • Needs you is split into Do now and Set up, and stays pinned while you chat.
  • n8n views show only for people with n8n access. Switch “View as” to compare.
  • “help”, “how do I start?”, “what is this app for?” or “list the steps to use this app” gets How to start: what the app is for, the steps from your words to the yes, and an example to try. “What can you do?” still lists what it can do.
  • Try typing “what needs my attention today?”, “is LangGraph up to date?”, “watch Kubernetes and email me”, “make it daily” or “pause the React check”.

Added on 16 Nov: the workflow drafter (ADR 0051)

  • “Draft a workflow that …” brings in the Workflow drafter. Each step shows as it goes, and the blocks appear on the line as it lays them out. Play “Draft a workflow”.
  • The draft card asks one question at a time, with choices from the team’s own data. A run time is never guessed. A guess shows as a guess you can change. Keys are never typed in the chat, and an Operator adds them in Settings.
  • Send for approval appears only when nothing is still needed. Until then the card says how many questions are left.
  • The report is code’s own words. In short comes first, then one row per section with a short gist. A row opens on a click, and Settings opens by itself while anything is still needed.
  • Where something can’t go on yet, the reason sits where its button would be, such as “Answer 1 question first”. No button is ever shown dead. The Planner’s set-up card and the change card follow the same rule.
  • Bob ticks each flag and says why. Approve appears only when every flag has a tick and a reason. A write by n8n’s own blocks won’t ask on each run, and the page says so. Play “Bob approves a draft”, then “Second Approver” for a workflow that changes who can use GitLab.
  • Explain sits on each row of Workflows, on the workflow page and on each version. “Explain the FastAPI release check” answers in the chat, with a Reworded label and code’s own wording one tap away. Play “Explain”.
  • A thumbs up or down sits under each Planner answer, only while tracing is on. Switch “Tracing” to compare.
